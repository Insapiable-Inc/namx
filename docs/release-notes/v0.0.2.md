NAM-X 0.0.2 adds ASIO on Windows: NAM-X can now play through your audio interface's own
ASIO driver, for the lowest latency your interface offers.

## What changed

- **ASIO on Windows.** In *Settings → Audio → Driver*, choose **ASIO** instead of WASAPI
  (WASAPI stays the default). *Input* then lists your interface's input channels (pick the
  one your guitar is on) and *Output* its output pairs, plus any other ASIO drivers
  installed; input and output always use one driver. *Driver settings…* opens the
  driver's own window, where ASIO sets its buffer size and usually the sample rate. Each
  driver remembers its own devices, so switching back to WASAPI brings the WASAPI ones
  back.
- NAM-X uses Steinberg's ASIO SDK under the GPLv3 option of its dual license, so the
  Windows builds are now distributed under GPL version 3 (NAM-X itself stays
  GPL-3.0-or-later). ASIO is a trademark of Steinberg Media Technologies GmbH.

## Tips for ASIO

- **Pick your interface's own driver.** Before you choose anything, NAM-X opens the first
  ASIO driver Windows lists. If you also have a generic one installed, such as Steinberg's
  "Generic Low Latency ASIO Driver" or ASIO4ALL, that may be the one: it runs on top of
  Windows audio and has more latency than WASAPI. Click *Out:* in the status bar and choose
  your interface's driver instead; NAM-X remembers it.
- **Set 48 kHz** in the driver's settings if your models are 48 kHz ones (most are), so
  NAM-X doesn't have to resample.
- **Buffer size:** 32–64 frames is plenty for most systems. Smaller still works, but costs
  much more CPU for a difference you can't hear.

## Downloads

- **Linux:** the AppImage below (`chmod +x` it and run it). It uses your system's JACK or
  PipeWire and needs glibc 2.39 or newer (Ubuntu 24.04, Debian 13, Fedora 40 and up).
- **Windows:** the installer `namx-0.0.2-windows-x64-setup.exe`, or the portable
  `namx-0.0.2-windows-x64.zip` (unzip, run `namx.exe`). Both are unsigned, so
  SmartScreen (and some browsers) warn about them; the release lists their SHA-256 sums.
- **Source:** the tarball below; build instructions are in `BUILD.md`.

The prebuilt binaries need an x86-64 CPU with AVX2 and FMA (Intel 2013+, AMD 2015+).

## Known limitations

- **ASIO:** unplugging and replugging the interface doesn't reconnect by itself — pick
  the device again or restart NAM-X. A buffer size changed in the driver's window may
  only apply after restarting NAM-X, if the driver doesn't announce the change.
- No macOS build, no DAW plugin.
- Mono models only (one input, one output).
- No distribution packages yet; on Linux it's the AppImage or a source build.
