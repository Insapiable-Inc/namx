What the existing measurements establish

The collection audit reproduced a 1.52 dB median gain-envelope error over 39 reused fitting takes, with larger errors on staircases and light picking. These are training residuals, not independent validation. The original NS-2 data include 61 takes across two sessions. The dynamics session's recordings named “bypass” were actually engaged and must not be used as calibration references. The first-session stairs-110 reports one xrun and is not the basis for this experiment.

The opposite histories differ substantially. At −56 dB on the way up, gain rises by 6.31 dB within the observed step; the existing fitted opening threshold at this knob position is about −48.11 dB. A threshold remapping may correct where the transition occurs, but cannot alone produce a slow rise during a perfectly constant input. The staircase confounds input level and prior state, and its last window is not proof of equilibrium.

The earlier noon-Threshold slow steps suggested roughly one-second rises at all three tested Decay positions. Their very low output levels and reused input history limit that inference. A fixed near-threshold time scale, charge left by prior signal, existing hysteresis with a miscalibrated threshold, and estimator bias near the noise floor remain distinguishable explanations. No circuit or component values are identified.

The subsequent history test, selected from the locator

Choose one level with the strongest measurable partial opening and a second level that opens promptly. Retain a third partial level only if the locator shows that two levels cannot separate the explanations. At each level, compare approximately eight seconds of constant input after a quiet reset with eight seconds at that identical level immediately following a two-second −12 dB conditioning tone. Repeat the identical packet at Decay minimum and maximum, with Threshold still untouched. The specific amplitudes and predictions must be saved before this second capture stage.

Reset duration is part of the experiment. At maximum Decay, the baseline's slow time constant is 460.3 ms × 7.94 = 3.655 seconds. After an eight-second reset, 11.2% of its prior level-state excess remains. Twenty-four seconds reduces this to about .14%. Therefore use an approximately 24-second low-input reset before each “quiet-history” probe, and check its adequacy rather than calling a short gap a fresh state. Use the same packet at both Decay settings to avoid confounding signal duration with control position. True zero samples can be used in the later generated packet; the physical interface noise remains part of the input.
